Among the CCSPP schools, what are the academic, social-emotional, health, and family supports available to students and families?

Overview of the Whole-Child and Family Supports Inventory in Year 4

Resources

Whole Child & Family Supports Inventory

  • Inventory Form

This section of the APR assesses the extent to which schools are providing academic, social-emotional, health, and family supports and services as a result of CCSPP implementation. The questions explore if the support was part of their implementation plan and how well these supports are coordinated and responsive to local needs and assets.

The 27 supports in the inventory are divided into six categories: Integrated Student Supports and Services; Community, Student & Family Engagement; Collaborative Leadership Practices; Expanded & Enriched Learning Time; Community-based Curriculum & Pedagogy, and; Positive & Restorative School Climate. Year 4 findings for each category are presented below.

I. Integrated Student Supports and Services

Highlights
  • Availability/Presence
  • Implementation of the Support/Service
  • Served individuals by Support/Service
  • Support/Service Funding

In Year 4, across Cohorts 1–4, the most widely available Integrated Student Support and Services were those identified as part of a Multi-Tiered System of Support (83%) and Mental Health Screening and Services (81%). Academic Support including tutoring and specialists was the third most commonly reported support, with 79% of schools reporting it as part of their CCSPP implementation.

Most schools were already providing Integrated Student Supports and Services when they applied for CCSPP funding and subsequently incorporated these existing supports into their community school implementation. For example, 89% of schools reported previously implementing Multi-Tiered Systems of Support, and 87% reported previously providing Mental Health Screening and Services.

CCSPP funding also supported the introduction of new services and structures. Notably, 26% of schools reported establishing a Coordination of Services Team (e.g., COST team) exclusively as a result of CCSPP funding.

Grantees were asked to report the number of individuals served by each support. In Year 4, Multi-Tiered Systems of Support reached an average of 407 students per school, while Mental Health Screening and Services reached an average of 241 students per school. Given that the average California public school serves approximately 550–600 students, these findings suggest that integrated student supports and services are reaching substantial numbers of students in CCSPP community schools.

To support the long-term sustainability of these services, grantees were asked to identify funding sources beyond CCSPP. Schools reported drawing on traditional funding sources, including the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) to support Multi-Tiered Systems of Support (33% of grantees) and Title I to fund Academic Supports (22%).

Mental Health Screening and Services, among the most widely available supports, relied on a more varied mix of funding sources. Schools reported using district funds (19%), LCFF funds (18%), and general funds (14%), with no single source predominating.

II. Community, Student, and Family Engagement

Highlights
  • Availability/Presence
  • Implementation of the Support/Services
  • Served individuals by Support/Service
  • Support/Service Funding

Among Community, Student, and Family Engagement supports and services, Multiple Modes of Family Communication and Involvement was the most widely implemented, reported by 87% of grantees. In Year 4, 65% of schools also reported conducting Home Visits.

Grantees reported incorporating student, family, and community engagement supports and services into their community school implementation. Notably, 35% of grantees indicated that their Adult Education programs were made possible exclusively through CCSPP funding.

Grantees reported the number of individuals served by each support. In Year 4, schools reported reaching parents and family members through a range of Community, Student, and Family Engagement supports and services, including family communication, home visits, and formal education opportunities.

Schools reported drawing on multiple funding sources to sustain Community, Student, and Family Engagement supports and services. For family communication and engagement, schools most commonly reported using LCFF (24%), general funds (23%), and district funds (19%), with similar patterns for Home Visits. In contrast, for Adult Education, “other” funding sources were the most frequently reported (9%).

III. Collaborative Leadership Practices

Highlights
  • Availability/Presence of the Support/Service
  • Implementation of the Support/Services
  • Served individuals by Support/Service
  • Support/Service Funding

Among supports and services related to Collaborative Leadership Practices, 86% of grantees reported establishing a shared decision-making body designed to center the voices of students, families, and other community members and interest-holders.

CCSPP funding supported the introduction of new Collaborative Leadership Practices. Notably, 30% of grantees reported establishing Parent Leadership Development Opportunities exclusively as a result of CCSPP funding, while 23% reported establishing shared decision-making bodies exclusively through CCSPP funding.

Grantees reported the number of individuals served by each support. In Year 4, schools reported reaching parents and family members through Collaborative Leadership Practices, including Parent Leadership Development Opportunities and shared decision-making bodies.

Schools reported drawing on multiple funding sources to support Collaborative Leadership Practices. Teacher Leadership Development and Opportunities were funded through a range of sources, including LCFF, general funds, and Title I. Similarly, newly implemented shared decision-making bodies were supported through LCFF, general funds, Title I, and district funds.

IV. Expanded and Enriched Learning Time

Highlights
  • Presence of the Supports/Services
  • Implementation of the Support/Services
  • Served individuals by Support/Service
  • Support/Service Funding

Among Expanded and Enriched Learning Time supports and services, After School programs were the most widely implemented, reported by 75% of grantees. More than 60% of schools also reported implementing Summer Programs and additional During School Programs.

CCSPP funding supported the introduction of new Expanded and Enriched Learning Time programs. Notably, 19% of grantees reported starting Before School programs exclusively as a result of CCSPP funding. In addition, at least 10% of schools offering After School and During School programs reported starting these programs exclusively through CCSPP funding.

Expanded and Enriched Learning Time programs are reaching substantial numbers of students. In Year 4, schools reported serving an average of 371 students through additional During School programs, 253 through After School programs, 228 through Before School programs, and 150 through Summer Programs. Given that California public schools enroll approximately 550–600 students on average, these figures suggest that expanded learning opportunities are reaching meaningful numbers of students in participating community schools.

The Expanded Learning Opportunities Program (ELOP) was the most commonly reported funding source for After School and Summer Programs. In Year 4, 54% of schools reported using ELOP funds to support After School programs, while 40% reported using these funds for Summer Programs.

V. Community-based Curriculum and Pedagogy

Highlights
  • Availability/Overview of the Presence of the Support/Service
  • Implementation of the Support/Services
  • Served individuals by Support/Service
  • Support/Service Funding

Among Community-Based Curriculum and Pedagogy Practices, 52% of grantees reported implementing Culturally Sustaining and Responsive Curriculum and Pedagogy, and 44% and 45% implementing project-based or community-based learning, respectively.

CCSPP funding supported the introduction of new Community-Based Curriculum and Pedagogy Practices. Notably, 30% of grantees reported implementing Community-Based Curriculum, Pedagogy, and Projects exclusively as a result of CCSPP funding, while 21% reported introducing Project-Based Learning with support from CCSPP.

Community-Based Curriculum and Pedagogy Practices are reaching substantial numbers of students. In Year 4, schools reported serving hundreds of students through these practices, with Advisory Systems alone reaching nearly 400 students per school on average. Given that California public schools enroll approximately 550–600 students on average, these findings suggest that community-based learning practices are reaching a substantial share of students in participating schools.

Schools reported drawing on a range of funding sources to support Community-Based Curriculum and Pedagogy Practices. In particular, general funds, LCFF, and district funds were used to support Project-Based Learning and Personalized Learning Plans.

VI. Positive and Restorative School Climate

Highlights
  • Availability/Overview of the Presence of the Support/Service
  • Implementation of the Support/Services
  • Served individuals by Support/Service
  • Support/Service Funding

Among Positive and Restorative School Climate supports and services, 84% of grantees reported implementing Programs and Practices that Teach Social-Emotional Skills, while 83% reported implementing Positive Behavioral Supports.

CCSPP funding supported the introduction of new Positive and Restorative School Climate practices. In Year 4, 15% of grantees reported implementing Programs and Practices that Teach Social-Emotional Skills exclusively as a result of CCSPP funding.

Programs and Practices that Teach Social-Emotional Skills are reaching substantial numbers of students. In Year 4, schools reported serving approximately 470 students per school on average through these programs and practices. Compared with the estimated statewide average enrollment of 550–600 students per school, this suggests that social-emotional learning supports are reaching large numbers of students in participating community schools.

Schools reported relying primarily on Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) funds to support Positive and Restorative School Climate practices. LCFF was the most commonly reported funding source for Positive Behavioral Supports (33% of schools), Practices that Prevent, Reduce, and Eliminate Exclusionary Discipline (26%), and Programs and Practices that Teach Social-Emotional Skills (30%).
