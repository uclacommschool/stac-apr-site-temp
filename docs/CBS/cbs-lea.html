Snapshot of Cohorts 1 through 4 in 2025 to 2026

In the Year 4 APR (2025-26), LEAs serving Cohorts 1-3 most often rated their implementation as Engaging across all five Capacity-Building Strategies (CBSs). Cohort 1 had the largest Transforming share in each strategy. Strategic Community Partnerships had the highest Transforming share within every cohort: 35.7%, 33.3%, 18.1%, and 11.1% in Cohorts 1-4, respectively.

Centering Community-Based Learning had the highest Visioning share within each cohort: 29.1%, 27.4%, 36.1%, and 61.1%, respectively. Cohort 4 had a majority in Visioning only for this strategy; Engaging was its largest category in the other four. These distributions identify community-based learning as a comparatively early area of implementation, which is consistent with the site level findings.

Implementation across the five strategies

The following excerpts illustrate reported practices and challenges across five different LEAs. They were selected for relevance to each strategy, not through systematic qualitative coding, and do not establish how common a practice is. Phase labels refer to each LEA’s rating of the strategy discussed.

Shared Commitment, Understanding, and Priorities

Engaging accounted for 64.3% of Cohort 1, 81.0% of Cohort 2, 80.7% of Cohort 3, and 61.1% of Cohort 4 ratings in 2025-26. Transforming was most common in Cohort 1 (28.6%), while 36.1% of Cohort 4 remained in Visioning. In the three-year matched panels, Visioning declined from 36.6% to 2.4% in Cohort 1 and from 60.0% to 4.3% in Cohort 2. The distributions suggest that shared priorities are increasingly being put into practice among continuing respondents.

With respect to Shared Commitment, Understanding, and Priorities, the CCSPP enabled us to conduct structured focus groups with school staff, students, and families across sites, while establishing an LEA Steering Committee that now meets regularly.

Cohort 4; Visioning for this strategy. 2025-26 APR, CBS reflection.

Collaborative Leadership

Engaging was the most common phase in all four cohorts in 2025-26. Transforming ranged from 27.3% in Cohort 1 and 17.9% in Cohort 2 to 9.6% in Cohort 3 and 2.8% in Cohort 4. Within the matched panels, Visioning declined from 41.5% to 9.8% in Cohort 1 and from 68.6% to 7.1% in Cohort 2 between 2023-24 and 2025-26. These ratings describe broader adoption of collaborative leadership, while the reflection below gives one example of the structures LEAs associate with this work.

Through Collaborative Leadership, we continued to strengthen shared decision-making structures such as School Site Councils, English Learner Advisory Councils, Community School committees, and site leadership teams.

Cohort 2; Transforming for this strategy. 2025-26 APR, CBS reflection.

The example connects reported leadership capacity to recurring councils and committees involving families, educators, and community members.

Centering Community-Based Learning

Community-based learning had the highest Visioning share within every cohort in 2025-26. Even among Cohorts 1 and 2, 29.1% and 27.4% of annual respondents remained in Visioning. There was nonetheless movement in both matched panels: Cohort 1 Transforming increased from 2.4% to 12.2%, while Cohort 2 Engaging rose from 28.6% to 71.4% over the three years. San Diego Unified described professional learning and compensated collaboration time as supports for instructional change.

The CCSPP has provided the resources necessary for crucial support positions, along with time for educators to create, innovate, plan, and collaborate.

Cohort 1, Cohort 2, Cohort 3; Engaging for this strategy. 2025-26 APR, CBS reflection.

The example highlights protected collaboration time and staff support as part of the district’s approach to developing community-based learning.

Sustaining Staff and Resources

Engaging was the largest category in all four cohorts, ranging from 54.2% in Cohort 4 to 77.4% in Cohort 2. Cohort 1 had the largest Transforming share (23.2%). The matched panels showed higher Transforming shares in 2025-26 than in 2023-24: from 2.4% to 19.5% in Cohort 1 and from 1.4% to 18.6% in Cohort 2. Annual distributions add nuance: Cohort 1 Visioning increased slightly from 11.5% to 12.5% between 2024-25 and 2025-26, even as its Transforming share increased. Different respondent composition can contribute to differences between the annual and matched results.

Budget uncertainty and staffing stability remained sustainability concerns, especially when CCSPP-funded positions such as Community School Managers served as the connective link for coordinating partnership, services, and leadership teams.

Cohort 1, Cohort 2, Cohort 3; Engaging for this strategy. 2025-26 APR, CBS reflection.

Oakland’s reflection cautions against treating active staffing and coordination as proof that long-term funding is already secure.

Strategic Community Partnerships

Partnerships had the highest Transforming share within every cohort in 2025-26. Engaging remained the largest category, including in Cohort 1 (60.7% Engaging versus 35.7% Transforming). In the matched panels, Transforming increased from 19.5% to 36.6% in Cohort 1 and from 5.7% to 38.6% in Cohort 2 over the three years. These are changes in the same LEAs’ self-assessments. One LEA described one way its partnership work was organized:

standardized partnership tracking; and QR-coded Resource Hubs at every campus connecting families to mental health, nutrition, housing, and postsecondary supports.

Cohort 3; Transforming for this strategy. 2025-26 APR, CBS reflection.

The example connects partnership coordination with visible routes for families to reach services and postsecondary supports.

Taken together, the ratings and examples suggest useful questions for technical assistance: how LEAs support community-based learning within instruction, how shared leadership structures distribute influence, and how staffing plans connect to sustained funding.

Longitudinal trends for Cohort 1

Figure 2 shows Cohort 1 LEA CBS ratings from all available valid responses in each year: 48 LEAs in 2023-24, 51-52 in 2024-25, and 55-56 in 2025-26. These are implementation years 2, 3, and 4 for Cohort 1. Because the group of responding LEAs changes from year to year, the figure describes the cohort as reported in each year, and year-to-year differences can reflect both changes in ratings and changes in who responded.

Between 2023-24 and 2025-26, the Visioning share declined and the Transforming share increased in all five strategies. Visioning fell from 37.5% to 7.1% in Shared Commitment, Understanding, and Priorities, from 41.7% to 10.9% in Collaborative Leadership, and from 25.0% to 3.6% in Strategic Community Partnerships. Transforming more than tripled in Shared Commitment (8.3% to 28.6%) and rose from 4.2% to 23.2% in Sustaining Staff and Resources. Strategic Community Partnerships remained the most advanced strategy by this measure, with Transforming increasing from 18.8% to 35.7%.

Most of the latest-year change occurred at the upper end of the scale. In 2024-25, Engaging accounted for 67.3% to 78.4% of ratings in four of the five strategies. From 2024-25 to 2025-26, Engaging declined and Transforming increased in every strategy. Sustaining Staff and Resources was the only strategy in which Visioning did not continue to decline, edging up from 11.5% to 12.5%. Centering Community-Based Learning progressed more slowly: Visioning declined from 52.1% to 29.1% over the three years but remained the highest Visioning share of any strategy in 2025-26, and Transforming reached 16.4%. Overall, the reported distribution moved toward later phases, although community-based learning remains a comparatively early area of implementation for this cohort.

Figure 2. Cohort 1 annual available-sample distributions. Changes may reflect both ratings and respondent composition.

Longitudinal trends for Cohort 2

Figure 3 shows all available valid Cohort 2 responses: 78 per item in 2023-24, 80-81 in 2024-25, and 84 in 2025-26. These are Cohort 2’s first three implementation years, so the figure follows the cohort from its start. As with Cohort 1, respondent membership varies across years, and differences can reflect both changes in ratings and changes in who responded.

In 2023-24, between 48.7% and 71.8% of ratings in each strategy were Visioning, and Transforming did not exceed 5.1% in any strategy. By 2025-26, Visioning had declined to below 10% in four strategies: Shared Commitment, Understanding, and Priorities (61.5% to 3.6%), Collaborative Leadership (67.9% to 9.5%), Sustaining Staff and Resources (48.7% to 4.8%), and Strategic Community Partnerships (48.7% to 3.6%). Engaging was the largest category in every strategy in 2025-26, ranging from 63.1% to 81.0%.

The timing of change differed across years. Between 2023-24 and 2024-25, movement was mainly from Visioning into Engaging, and Transforming remained at or below 10% in every strategy. Transforming grew mainly in the latest year: from 9.9% to 33.3% in Strategic Community Partnerships, from 7.4% to 17.9% in Collaborative Leadership, and from 1.2% to 17.9% in Sustaining Staff and Resources. Centering Community-Based Learning moved more slowly. Visioning declined from 71.8% to 27.4% and Engaging rose from 26.9% to 67.9%, but Transforming reached only 4.8% in 2025-26, so progress in this strategy mostly reflects movement into Engaging. In 2025-26, Cohort 2’s Transforming shares were lower than Cohort 1’s in every strategy; that year was Cohort 2’s third implementation year and Cohort 1’s fourth.
